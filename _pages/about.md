I am a third year undergraduate student majoring in Physics and Mathematics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT). I am interested in theoretical particle physics, in particular, I am drawn to how various phenomenological descriptions, including effective field theories and holography based models, connect theory, intuition, and data.

As of Summer 2026, I am working on a project studying jet substructure observable modification in light- and heavy-ion collisions using the hybrid strong/weak coupling model. I work closely with  Arjun Kudinoor, Prof. Krishna Rajagopal, and Prof. Daniel Pablos. We recently put out the first jet substructure calculations --- Soft-Drop $R_g$ and energy-energy correlator (EEC) --- in oxygen-oxygen collisions at the LHC. Using these substructure observables, we explore how strongly coupled energy loss, jet-induced wake, and Molière scattering modify the internal structure of jets in QGP droplets formed in OO collisions and probe the microscopic, quasiparticle structure of the QGP. From June - August 2025, I worked on a project at Technische Universität München (TUM), with Tom Magorsch, Prof. Nora Brambilla, and Prof. Antonio Vairo. This project is centered around improving the theoretical descriptions and numerical simulations of heavy quarkonia decay using the open quantum system + pNRQCD framework. We are currently making the finals edits to the paper.

Before coming to MIT, I participated in the International Physics Olympiad and Asian Physics Olympiad, representing Taiwan. In my second year of high school, I came in 11th in the 2022 International Physics Olympiad. After competing in IPhO, I started tutoring high school students to promote physics learning through a problem-solving based approach. As a continuation of such effort, I joined the MIT physics mentorship program in Spring 2025. Since then, I advised 6 students who were attending the first year electromagnetism classes (8.02 and 8.022) and 3 students attending the undergraduate statistical physics class (8.044) at MIT. I plan to keep contributing to the mentorship program to assist more students with understanding physics concepts and navigating the Institute, as well as to become a useful resource for other mentors and the mentorship program community.

I am a setter at MIT men's volleyball club, a proud fried chicken/mushroom chef at MIT Association of Taiwanese Students, and a slow braised dishes enjoyer. I also enjoy learning new things about different cultural traditions and languages. I speak Mandarin and Taiwanese Hokkien (a.k.a Taiwanese/Taiwan Minanese) as a native speaker, English is the main language I use in professional settings, and I have been learning German for roughly two years and can hold a liesure conversation in it.
