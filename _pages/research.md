## Overview
I am interested in theoretical particle physics, especially non-perturabtive methods in quantum field theories. In particular, I find the phenomenology of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) intriguing, and that they often lead to surprising connections between different fields of physics and exciting discoveries. On the practical aspect, I've enjoyed working on hard probes of the quark gluon plasma (QGP), applying phenomenological models such as non-relativistic effective field theories and the AdS/CFT dualituy to provide pre/postdictions of observable measurements in heavy ion collisions. 

## Jet substructure modification 
Since Fall 2025, I have been working on projects predicting jet substructure modification in quark gluon plasma formed in heavy- and light-ion collisions. In this project, I work closely with Arjun Kudinoor, Prof. Krishna Rajagopal , and Prof. Daniel Pablos, employing the strong/weak coupling model (Hybrid Model) of jet quenching and performing jet observable calculations. We analyze how energy loss, jet-induced wake, and hard Molière scattering modify jet observables such as the nuclear modification factor $R_{AA}$, the Soft Drop angle $R_g$, and the energy correlators. In this prject, I am primarily responsible for calculating two point energy-energy correlators (EECs) of jets formed in oxygen-oxygen collisions at the LHC, as well as building simplified models to understand features of the EECs better. Recently, we found that large radii jets receive enhancements at large angles in both $R_g$ and EEC when Molière scattering is included in the model. The behavior of these two observables could provide a promising avenue through which to detect and study Molière scattering in QGP.  

## Quarkonia suppression
Since June 2025, I have been studying heavy quarkonia decay in the QGP with Prof. Nora Brambilla, Prof. Antonio Vairo, and Tom Magorsch at Technische Universität München (TUM). In the past, open quantum systems and potential non-relativistic QCD (pNRQCD) were combined to derive a set of Redfield master equations of quarkonia dynamics. By exploiting temperature and energy hierarchies in the QGP, the Redfield master equations can be expanded to a Lindblad form, projected to radial space, and solved numerically. In this project, we work on implementing a novel numerical approach to relax the binding energy over temperature expansion, hoping to capture quarkonia dynamics in the QGP in wider range of temperatures. In our work, we first realized a general prescription to perform the coordinate transformation to radial space without an expansion. Then, we utilize unitary equivalence of quantum operations to rotate and minimize non-positive parts of the Redfield evolution and obtain a numerical recipe to restore positivity of the master equation without relying on the high temperature approximation. We then combined both theoretical motivations and lattice QCD data to improve the previous parameterization of chromoelectric correlators and simulated the full quarkonia dynamics using the full correlators. Tom also inspired me to think about possible ways to apply quantum computing and neural networks in high energy physics, but that is saved for the future.
